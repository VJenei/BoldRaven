Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 523210

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 523210, Securities and Commodity Exchanges, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    An exchange outage stops price discovery for everyone.

    WHY
    Exchanges operate matching engines, market data distribution, and clearing interfaces that the entire market depends on. When an exchange halts, participants cannot trade, hedge, or value positions, and derivative markets referencing those prices are affected simultaneously.
    LOSS
    Market-wide disruption with participant claims, SEC or CFTC investigation under system compliance and integrity requirements, and lasting damage to the exchange's competitive position against alternative venues.
    ASK
    What happens to participants who cannot hedge while your market is halted?
  2. 02

    Regulation SCI imposes specific systems requirements on covered entities.

    WHY
    Covered exchanges and clearing agencies must have policies reasonably designed to ensure their systems have adequate capacity, integrity, resiliency, availability, and security, must conduct business continuity testing with members, and must report systems disruptions and intrusions to the SEC.
    LOSS
    SEC enforcement for Regulation SCI failures, which examines the adequacy of the policies themselves rather than only the outcome, and requires notification and remediation reporting.
    ASK
    Are you a Regulation SCI entity, and when did you last conduct required resiliency testing?
  3. 03

    Order flow and market data reveal participant positions.

    WHY
    Exchanges see order flow, cancellations, and positions before and beyond what is publicly disseminated. That information reveals participant strategies and positions and is strictly controlled because access to it confers a trading advantage.
    LOSS
    Exposure of non-public order flow data undermines confidence in market fairness, brings regulatory scrutiny of information barriers, and drives participants to competing venues.
    ASK
    How is access to non-public order flow data controlled and audited?
