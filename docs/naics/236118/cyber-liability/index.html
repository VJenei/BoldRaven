Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 236118

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 236118, Residential Remodelers, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    You hold homeowner payment details, alarm codes, and access information.

    WHY
    Remodelers keep customer names, addresses, payment cards or bank details for progress payments, and practical access information such as lockbox codes, alarm codes, and gate codes for occupied homes. Photographs of interiors are stored with the project file.
    LOSS
    A breach that exposes home addresses together with alarm codes and interior photographs creates a physical security risk to customers, which is a materially different and more serious claim than ordinary data loss.
    ASK
    Do you store customer alarm codes, lockbox codes, or interior photographs?
  2. 02

    Deposits and progress payments are collected from consumers.

    WHY
    Remodelling is paid in deposits and progress payments, often by card or bank transfer, arranged by email and text with the homeowner. Many states regulate the size of deposits a home improvement contractor may take, and registration is required.
    LOSS
    Diverted customer payments where the homeowner paid a fraudster using your name. The customer expects the work regardless, and state home improvement regulators treat deposit disputes seriously.
    ASK
    How do customers pay their deposit and progress payments?
  3. 03

    Estimates, contracts, and change orders are the entire legal record.

    WHY
    Residential remodelling generates constant scope change, agreed verbally on site and confirmed by text or email. State home improvement statutes typically require written contracts and written change orders, and consumer protection claims turn on whether the paperwork exists.
    LOSS
    A consumer claim you cannot defend without the written record, in a regulatory regime that is deliberately weighted toward the homeowner. Attorney fee shifting is common in home improvement statutes.
    ASK
    Where are your signed contracts and written change orders kept?
