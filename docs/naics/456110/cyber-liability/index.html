Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 456110

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 456110, Pharmacies and Drug Retailers, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    When the claims clearinghouse went down, pharmacies could not dispense.

    WHY
    Pharmacies adjudicate insurance claims in real time at the point of sale. The February 2024 ransomware attack on Change Healthcare, which processes a very large share of US healthcare transactions, left pharmacies nationwide unable to verify coverage or process claims for weeks, forcing cash payment or manual workarounds. The associated breach affected approximately 190 million people, the largest healthcare data breach in US history.
    LOSS
    An inability to adjudicate claims means either dispensing without knowing you will be paid or turning patients away. Contingent business interruption from a clearinghouse or PBM outage is a cyber policy coverage, not a property one.
    ASK
    Which clearinghouse and PBM do you depend on to adjudicate claims in real time?
  2. 02

    You are a HIPAA covered entity holding complete patient medication histories.

    WHY
    Pharmacies hold patient identifiers, prescriber details, diagnoses inferable from therapy, insurance information, and complete dispensing histories. As a covered entity you carry direct breach notification duties to patients, HHS, and for large breaches the media, plus civil monetary penalty exposure.
    LOSS
    HIPAA breach notification and OCR enforcement, with medication history being among the most sensitive categories of personal data because it reveals conditions patients have not disclosed to anyone else.
    ASK
    How many patient records with dispensing history are held in your system?
  3. 03

    Controlled substance and DSCSA recordkeeping are separate federal duties.

    WHY
    DEA registration requires perpetual controlled substance records, biennial inventories, and reporting of losses. Separately, under DSCSA dispensers must receive, store, and provide electronic transaction information, with the compliance date for dispensers of November 27, 2025 and a limited exemption for dispensers with 25 or fewer full-time employees until November 27, 2026.
    LOSS
    DEA action for unreconcilable controlled substance records, and inability to lawfully receive product where DSCSA transaction data cannot be exchanged or retained.
    ASK
    How do you receive and store DSCSA transaction information for product you take in?
