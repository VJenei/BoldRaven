Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 115115

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 115115, Farm Labor Contractors and Crew Leaders, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    The business holds identity documents and payroll data for large numbers of workers.

    WHY
    Farm labor contractors maintain I-9 forms, passport and visa copies, consular documentation, Social Security numbers, US and foreign addresses, direct deposit details, and weekly payroll for hundreds or thousands of workers, many of whom are H-2A foreign nationals. The workforce turns over multiple times per year.
    LOSS
    A breach requires notification in every state of residence, credit monitoring, and response to the Department of Labor and state agencies. For a workforce of 1,000 or more, costs commonly reach six or seven figures before litigation.
    ASK
    How many workers do you hold identity documents and Social Security numbers for?
  2. 02

    Growers contract with the FLC to carry labor compliance responsibility.

    WHY
    MSPA registration, joint employer doctrine, and grower contracts place responsibility for wage and hour compliance, worker verification, transportation, and housing documentation on the contractor. Hours, piece rates, transportation logs, and housing inspection records are the evidence in any claim and are held electronically.
    LOSS
    In a wage and hour claim, an employer who cannot produce time and pay records faces a legal presumption in favor of the employee's estimate, applied across the workforce.
    ASK
    How do you record and store hours worked and piece rates for each worker?
  3. 03

    Weekly payroll for a large workforce is processed on a fixed deadline.

    WHY
    Farm labor contractors process large weekly payrolls with direct deposit files, tax deposits, and grower invoicing. This is high-volume, deadline-driven payment activity arranged by email with clients and banks. Payroll diversion, where direct deposit details are altered, is a common attack.
    LOSS
    Diverted payroll funds or altered deposit files, plus crews that do not return to work when payment is missed while the grower's crop is in the field.
    ASK
    What is your weekly payroll amount, and how is your direct deposit file transmitted?
