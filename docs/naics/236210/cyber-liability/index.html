Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 236210

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 236210, Industrial Building Construction, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    You hold drawings and layouts of your clients' production facilities.

    WHY
    Industrial builders work from process layouts, utility routing, structural drawings, and equipment arrangements that describe how a client's plant operates. For food, pharmaceutical, chemical, or defence clients this is confidential and in some cases regulated information.
    LOSS
    Third-party liability to the client for disclosed facility information, plus loss of a relationship in a sector where prequalification takes years. For chemical facilities the information itself may be security sensitive.
    ASK
    How many clients' facility drawings are stored on your systems?
  2. 02

    Federal and defence-related work carries contractual cyber requirements.

    WHY
    Contractors handling federal contract information must meet the basic safeguarding requirements of FAR 52.204-21, and those handling controlled unclassified information face DFARS and CMMC obligations. These flow down through subcontracts. Failure to meet them is a contract compliance failure and, under the False Claims Act, potentially more.
    LOSS
    Loss of eligibility for federal work, contract termination, and False Claims Act exposure where compliance was represented but not achieved.
    ASK
    Do any of your contracts flow down federal cybersecurity clauses such as FAR 52.204-21 or DFARS?
  3. 03

    Industrial projects carry the largest payment applications and the tightest schedules.

    WHY
    Plant construction and expansion is scheduled around a client production outage window that cannot move. Payment applications run into millions monthly. Both the schedule and the money flow through project management and accounting systems.
    LOSS
    A missed outage window forces the client to run without the new capacity for another cycle, with consequential damages claimed against the contractor, and diverted payment applications in the seven figure range.
    ASK
    Are your projects tied to a client shutdown window that cannot be rescheduled?
