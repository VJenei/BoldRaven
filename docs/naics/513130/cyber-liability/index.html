Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 513130

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 513130, Book Publishers, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    Unpublished manuscripts have been stolen through targeted phishing for years.

    WHY
    A sustained campaign impersonating editors, agents, and scouts obtained unpublished manuscripts from publishers, agencies, and authors over several years, leading to a federal indictment in 2022. Manuscripts move by email between author, agent, editor, and production long before publication.
    LOSS
    A manuscript leak before publication undermines the launch, exposes the publisher to author claims, and in a business built on author trust, the reputational damage affects acquisition of future titles.
    ASK
    How do editors verify that a manuscript request genuinely comes from the person it claims?
  2. 02

    Publication dates are announced, marketed, and immovable.

    WHY
    Books are announced to retailers, reviewers, and the trade months ahead, with print runs, distribution, and marketing committed to a date. Production files must reach the printer on schedule for physical books to exist on publication day.
    LOSS
    A missed publication date with retail placement, review coverage, and author publicity already scheduled, none of which can be recreated for a later date.
    ASK
    How far ahead of publication are your print and distribution commitments made?
  3. 03

    Author contracts and royalty accounting run for decades.

    WHY
    Publishing agreements govern rights, territories, subsidiary licensing, and royalties over the life of copyright, with advances recouped and statements issued semi-annually. Authors hold audit rights over that accounting.
    LOSS
    Unreconcilable royalty accounts across a backlist spanning decades, triggering author audits with cost-shifting provisions and disputes over rights that cannot be resolved without the contract records.
    ASK
    How many years of author contracts and royalty records do you maintain?
