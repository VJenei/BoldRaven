Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 115112

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 115112, Soil Preparation, Planting, and Cultivating, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    The business holds field data belonging to many grower clients.

    WHY
    Custom applicators and planting contractors receive clients' field boundaries, soil test results, variable-rate prescriptions, yield history, and application records. This data is held for many growers at once on the contractor's systems.
    LOSS
    Third-party liability to grower clients whose agronomic and financial data was exposed. A property policy does not respond because the loss is another party's information.
    ASK
    How many grower clients' field data do you store on your systems?
  2. 02

    Prescription files are executed automatically across large acreages.

    WHY
    Variable-rate application runs from prescription files loaded into rate controllers and section control systems. A wrong or corrupted file applies the wrong product or rate across the field. The result is not visible for days or weeks.
    LOSS
    Crop damage claims from misapplication, plus off-target drift claims from neighboring property. Herbicide injury claims on row crops commonly run $200 to $800 per acre.
    ASK
    How are prescription files transferred to your equipment, and who verifies them?
  3. 03

    Pesticide application records are legally required and are the primary defense in a claim.

    WHY
    Federal law requires restricted use pesticide application records to be kept for two years. State requirements are often stricter with shorter reporting deadlines. These records are the primary evidence in a drift complaint, worker exposure claim, or residue investigation, and are usually electronic.
    LOSS
    Regulatory penalties for missing records, and inability to defend a drift or exposure claim without documentation of what was applied, when, and at what rate.
    ASK
    How and where do you store your restricted use pesticide application records?
