Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 561450

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 561450, Credit Bureaus, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    This industry produced one of the largest and most consequential breaches in history.

    WHY
    The Equifax breach disclosed in 2017 exposed personal information of approximately 147 million people and led to a settlement with the FTC, CFPB, and states of up to $700 million. Credit bureaus hold the most complete financial identity records that exist about individuals who never chose to provide them.
    LOSS
    Notification and remediation across a population measured in the tens or hundreds of millions, with FTC, CFPB, and state enforcement, class litigation, and congressional attention. This sector's breaches define the upper bound of data breach cost.
    ASK
    How many consumer files does your organisation hold?
  2. 02

    The Fair Credit Reporting Act governs everything you do with the data.

    WHY
    Consumer reporting agencies must maintain accuracy, provide disclosures, investigate disputes, and limit furnishing to permissible purposes, with private rights of action and statutory and punitive damages. Reasonable procedures to protect against improper access are required.
    LOSS
    FCRA claims with statutory and punitive damages, brought individually and as classes, plus regulatory enforcement examining whether procedures to prevent improper access were reasonable.
    ASK
    How do you verify that a subscriber pulling a report has a permissible purpose?
  3. 03

    Subscriber credentials are used to pull reports fraudulently.

    WHY
    Access is provided to lenders, landlords, and employers through subscriber accounts. Compromised or fraudulently obtained subscriber credentials have repeatedly been used to pull consumer reports for identity theft, which is a documented pattern in this industry.
    LOSS
    Consumer files accessed through legitimate-looking subscriber accounts, with the bureau responsible for the improper access and the consumers affected suffering identity theft they cannot trace.
    ASK
    How do you detect a subscriber account being used for improper access?
