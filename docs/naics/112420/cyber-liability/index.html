Cyber Liability Insurance for NAICS 112420

CYB

Ranked reasons a business in NAICS 112420, Goat Farming, needs Cyber Liability coverage. Each reason is specific to how this industry actually loses money, and ends with the question to ask before the objection comes up.

  1. 01

    Dairy goat operations are regulated under the Pasteurized Milk Ordinance.

    WHY
    Grade A goat milk requires cooling to 45 degrees F within two hours, automated tank monitoring, CIP cycles, and testing on every pickup. Operations that process on-farm into cheese also carry FDA facility registration, process controls, and pathogen testing records.
    LOSS
    Dumped milk, rejected loads, and possible permit suspension. On-farm cheese operations face recall exposure if pathogen and process records cannot be produced.
    ASK
    Do you ship Grade A milk or process dairy products on the farm?
  2. 02

    Meat and breeding stock sales are concentrated around holiday demand.

    WHY
    Meat goats sell into ethnic and holiday markets with demand concentrated around religious holidays, often through online auctions and long-distance buyers paid by wire or payment app. Registered breeding stock sells nationally through online sales.
    LOSS
    Payment fraud during a small number of high-demand sale windows, and diverted proceeds on breeding stock transactions of $1,000 to $10,000 per animal.
    ASK
    How much of your annual sales occurs during holiday demand periods?
  3. 03

    Buyers require disease testing records before purchase.

    WHY
    Goats are covered by the National Scrapie Eradication Program with identification and movement record requirements for interstate commerce. Buyers also require CAE, CL, and Johne's testing records before purchasing breeding stock.
    LOSS
    Inability to produce disease testing records reduces breeding stock value to commercial meat value and blocks interstate movement.
    ASK
    Where do you keep your herd disease testing results?
